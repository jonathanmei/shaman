Non-uniform rank allocation: what the depth ramp and the type weights do, and why the full budget lost to parity

A short note on the rank allocator added on branch kd-norms-fact-stab (utils.calculate_ranks / allocate_ranks): the per-layer bit multipliers, the budget matching, the Qwen3-0.6B result, and two hypotheses for the parity-versus-full gap.

NanoQuant · Qwen3 · 1.0 bpw · 9 September 2026

1. Setup: from a bit budget to a rank

Layer \(\ell\) sits in decoder block \(b_\ell \in \{0,\dots,B-1\}\), has shape \(a_\ell \times n_\ell\) (out × in) and \(N_\ell = a_\ell n_\ell\) weights. With the two 16-bit scale vectors of the paper's export, a rank-\(r\) factorisation costs

\[ \operatorname{bits}_\ell(r) \;=\; r\,(a_\ell + n_\ell) \;+\; 16\,(a_\ell + n_\ell), \tag{1} \]

so a per-layer budget of \(\beta_\ell\) bits per weight corresponds to the continuous rank

\[ r^\star_\ell \;=\; \frac{\beta_\ell\, N_\ell}{a_\ell + n_\ell} \;-\; 16 . \tag{2} \]

The paper's rule (Appendix F) is \(\beta_\ell = \beta = 1.0\) for every layer, followed by flooring \(r^\star_\ell\) to a multiple of 32. The flooring is why "1.0 bpw" is 0.973 bpw at 0.6B and 0.986 at 1.7B/4B: about 1.4–2.7 % of the budget is never spent.

2. Ramp and type: a multiplier on the per-layer budget

Both levers are a single multiplicative factor on \(\beta\):

\[ \beta_\ell \;=\; \beta\, c\, m_\ell, \qquad m_\ell \;=\; \underbrace{\exp\!\Big(\rho\,\big(\tfrac{b_\ell}{B-1} - \tfrac12\big)\Big)}_{\text{depth ramp}} \;\cdot\; \underbrace{w_{t(\ell)}}_{\text{type weight}}, \qquad c \;=\; \frac{\sum_\ell N_\ell}{\sum_\ell m_\ell N_\ell}. \tag{3} \]

Depth ramp \(\rho\)

A geometric ramp over depth, symmetric in log space around the middle block, so \(\rho\) is the log-ratio of the last block's budget to the first block's. With \(\rho = 0.6\) the first block gets \(e^{-0.3} \approx 0.74\times\) and the last block \(e^{0.3} \approx 1.35\times\) the mean budget, a ratio of \(e^{0.6} \approx 1.8\). Motivation: in the 4B diag run the last 8 of 36 blocks produced half of the pre-KD perplexity damage (0.49 PPL per block versus 0.14 for blocks 0–27), while every block had the same rank.

Type weights \(w_t\)

A fixed multiplier per sub-layer type \(t(\ell) \in \{\)q, k, v, o, gate, up, down\(\}\), set from the median relative block-loss jump caused by binarising that layer type over the 27 logged 4B blocks:

The multipliers only move bits, they never add them. The constant \(c\) in (3) renormalises so that \(\sum_\ell \beta_\ell N_\ell = \beta \sum_\ell N_\ell\): the continuous budgets sum to exactly the uniform rule's target, whatever \(\rho\) and \(w\) are.

3. Budget matching in steps of 32

Each rank starts at \(r^\star_\ell\) of (2) floored to a multiple of 32 and clamped to \([32, \min(a_\ell, n_\ell)]\). The allocator then moves ranks in \(\pm 32\) steps toward a total-bit target \(T\), always choosing the layer with the largest relative excess

\[ \varepsilon_\ell \;=\; \frac{r_\ell - r^\star_\ell}{r^\star_\ell} \tag{4} \]

to shrink when over budget, and the layer with the most negative \(\varepsilon_\ell\) (whose step still fits) to grow when under budget. Two targets are offered:

  • parity — \(T\) is the number of bits the uniform rule actually spends after its own flooring (0.9729 bpw at 0.6B), so arms are comparable at identical actual bpw;
  • full — \(T = \beta \sum_\ell N_\ell\), i.e. exactly 1.0 bpw, spending the flooring remainder.

5. Why did the full budget (24.15) lose to parity (23.25) with 2.7 % more bits?

Diffing the two allocations for 0.6B: full differs from parity in 120 of the 196 layers, each by one 32-step upward. Where those steps went is the first hypothesis.

Hypothesis 1: the remainder landed where the marginal bit is worth least

The fill rule (4) is sensitivity-blind. It grows whichever layer has the most negative \(\varepsilon_\ell\), i.e. the layer whose flooring lost the most relative to its target. That systematically favours

  • small-rank layers, where 32 is a large fraction of \(r^\star_\ell\): 22 k-projections and 20 q-projections grew, against 12 down-projections;
  • early blocks, where the ramp pushed \(r^\star_\ell\) down: 5–7 layers grew in each of blocks 0–11 versus 1–3 in blocks 25–27;
  • and it cannot grow the late-block MLP layers at all, because up and down are already capped at \(\min(a_\ell, n_\ell) = 1024\) there.

So the extra 2.7 % of bits went preferentially to q/k in early blocks — the layers and depths the ramp and type weights had just judged least valuable. The block trajectory agrees: full led parity early (block 0: 21.2 vs 23.6) and had lost by block 27 (25.08 vs 24.19). The direct test is a sensitivity-weighted fill (grow the layer with the largest \(m_\ell\)-weighted deficit, or restrict the remainder to the last third of the blocks).

Hypothesis 2: single-run noise

Run-to-run spread of a full 0.6B reconstruction is about \(\pm 0.5\) PPL. The two arms differ in 120 layers' ADMM problems and therefore in every block checkpoint, so they are effectively independent draws; a 0.9 gap is roughly two sigma. The ordering control \(>\) ramp \(\approx\) type \(>\) both is well outside that spread, the parity-versus-full gap is not, and a second seed would settle it.

A smaller third possibility

More rank in an early block changes its ADMM solution and hence the quantised-prefix activations that every later layer is tuned against. Under the sequential seven-stages-per-block schedule, extra early capacity is not a free improvement: the later blocks re-fit to a different prefix, and the end-to-end effect can trade against them.
